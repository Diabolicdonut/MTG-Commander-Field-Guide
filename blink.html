3 · Game plan

How to sequence the deck

Use a high density of cheap ETB creatures and blink effects that protect as well as generate value. Avoid loading the deck with seven-mana targets only.

Commander dependence test. Remove the commander twice in a practice game. A well-built list should still ramp, draw, interact, and advance its core theme.

Balanced Example — Brago, King EternalAtlas-curated reference · Bracket 3 · 100 cards+

A value-oriented Azorius blink deck. It uses inexpensive enter-the-battlefield creatures, a moderate counterspell package, and Brago combat triggers to accumulate cards and mana without leaning on a fast deterministic loop.

6 · Bracket tuning

Move the same idea up or down responsibly

Bracket 2

Remove Game Changers, fast mana, compact infinites, and efficient universal tutors. Favor visible board development.

Bracket 3

Allow up to three Game Changers, stronger engines, and one-turn finishes that require accumulated resources.

Bracket 4

Maximize speed and consistency without claiming to be a cEDH metagame deck. Use efficient tutors, free interaction, and compact wins.

7 · Wins and weaknesses

How games end—and how the deck is stopped

Common finishes

  • ETB value avalanche
  • Token combat
  • Blink-based infinite loop

Structural weaknesses

  • Torpor Orb effects
  • Board wipes
  • Long trigger stacks and sequencing errors
8 · Table expectations

The multiplayer part of deck construction

Blink turns can become repetitive. Demonstrate loops clearly and shortcut repeated triggers once opponents understand the result.
